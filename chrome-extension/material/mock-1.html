Listening · 4 parts · 40 questions · about 30 minutes

The recordings are read aloud by your browser's voices: Microsoft Edge uses its natural voices (needs internet), other browsers sound more robotic. The rules are the same as the real thing: you hear each part once, and you answer while you listen. Use headphones. Your answers save automatically.

Reading · 3 passages · 40 questions · 60 minutes

There's no extra transfer time, so write your answers straight in. Spelling counts. The timer submits automatically at 0:00.

Writing · 2 tasks · 60 minutes

Spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and 40 on Task 2. Task 2 is worth twice as much. Your drafts save automatically.

Writing Task 1

The chart below shows the percentage of commuters in the city of Greenford who used five different ways of travelling to work in 2005 and 2025.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.

Writing Task 2

Write about the following topic:

In many countries, a growing number of young people are choosing to study at universities abroad.
Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

Check yourself against the band descriptors

  • Task 1: Is there a clear overview, a sentence saying what changed most overall, with no numbers in it? Did you group the data (rose vs fell) instead of describing all five one by one? Are the figures accurate, and did you use "percentage points" for differences between percentages?
  • Task 2: Do you answer the question that was asked ("do the advantages outweigh…") and say so clearly in both the introduction and the conclusion? Does each body paragraph have one main idea, developed with an explanation or example?
  • Both: varied linking (not "Firstly… Secondly… Moreover" in every paragraph), accurate complex sentences, and no memorised phrases.

Speaking · 3 parts · 11–14 minutes

Record yourself on your phone, then play it back and mark it with the speaking band descriptors. Listen for fillers, long pauses and repeated words. Better still, ask someone to read the examiner's questions to you.

Part 1 · Introduction and interview (4–5 minutes)

Part 2 · Long turn (3–4 minutes)

Describe a skill you learned that took you a long time to master.

You should say:

  • what the skill was
  • when and why you started learning it
  • what made it difficult

and explain how you felt when you finally mastered it.

Examiner's follow-up: Do you still use this skill today?
